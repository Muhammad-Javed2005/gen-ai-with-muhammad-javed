===================================================================
                COVARIANCE VS. CORRELATION IN STATISTICS
===================================================================

1. OVERVIEW & DEFINITIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Both Covariance and Correlation measure the relationship and linear 
dependence between two random variables (X and Y).

* COVARIANCE:
  Measures the DIRECTION of the linear relationship between two 
  variables. It indicates whether two variables increase or 
  decrease together.

* CORRELATION:
  Measures both the DIRECTION and the STRENGTH of the linear 
  relationship between two variables. It is the standardized version 
  of covariance.


2. KEY DIFFERENCES AT A GLANCE
-------------------------------------------------------------------
PROPERTY                COVARIANCE                   CORRELATION
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Definition              Measures directional        Measures direction 
                        relationship                 AND strength
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Values / Range          -Infinity to +Infinity       -1.0 to +1.0
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Units                   Product of units of X & Y    Dimensionless 
                        (e.g., kg * meters)          (unitless ratio)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Effect of Scale         Sensitive to scale           Independent of 
                        (changing units changes      scale (unit invariant)
                        the value)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Primary Purpose         Shows direction only         Shows strength & 
                                                     comparable relationship
-------------------------------------------------------------------


3. MATHEMATICAL FORMULAS
-------------------------------------------------------------------
* Population Covariance:
  Cov(X, Y) = Σ [ (X_i - μ_X) * (Y_i - μ_Y) ] / N

* Sample Covariance:
  Cov(X, Y) = Σ [ (X_i - x̄) * (Y_i - ȳ) ] / (n - 1)

* Pearson Correlation Coefficient (r or ρ):
  ρ(X, Y) = Cov(X, Y) / (σ_X * σ_Y)

  Where:
  - μ_X, μ_Y = Population means of X and Y
  - x̄, ȳ   = Sample means of X and Y
  - σ_X, σ_Y = Standard deviations of X and Y
  - N, n     = Total number of observations


4. INTERPRETATION OF RESULTS
-------------------------------------------------------------------
COVARIANCE INTERPRETATION:
  * Cov(X, Y) > 0 : Positive relationship (Variables move together)
  * Cov(X, Y) < 0 : Negative relationship (Variables move oppositely)
  * Cov(X, Y) = 0 : No linear relationship

CORRELATION INTERPRETATION:
  * r = +1.0 : Perfect positive linear relationship
  * 0.7 <= r < 1.0 : Strong positive relationship
  * 0.3 <= r < 0.7 : Moderate positive relationship
  * 0.0 < r < 0.3 : Weak positive relationship
  * r = 0.0  : No linear relationship
  * r = -1.0 : Perfect negative linear relationship


5. PRACTICAL EXAMPLES
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Example 1: Height (cm) vs. Weight (kg)
- Covariance: +150 cm*kg (Hard to assess if 150 is large or small)
- Correlation: +0.85 (Clear indication of a strong positive relation)

Example 2: Unit Change Effect
- Height in meters vs. Weight in kg: Covariance drops from 150 to 1.5.
- Correlation remains strictly +0.85 regardless of measurement units.


6. SUMMARY & WHEN TO USE WHICH
-------------------------------------------------------------------
- Use COVARIANCE when you need to calculate intermediate steps in 
  statistical algorithms (e.g., Principal Component Analysis, 
  Portfolio Risk in Finance).

- Use CORRELATION when you want to interpret, summarize, or compare 
  relationships across different datasets or varying units of measurement.
